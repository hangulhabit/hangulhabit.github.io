About

Why I built Hangul Habit

Short version: I kept meeting people who loved Korean shows, music, and food — and wanted to actually speak the language, not just recognize a few words in subtitles. Most options felt too big, too slow, or too impersonal for that. So I built something smaller instead.

Why Korean, why now

Korean film, TV, music, and food have traveled further than most people expected a decade ago. A lot of that reaches people as entertainment first — and for a good number of them, curiosity grows into wanting to actually understand and speak Korean, not just watch it with subtitles on.

The gap I kept seeing: plenty of people reach that point, open a language app, do a lesson or two, and quietly stop. Not because they weren't motivated — because a few minutes of solo tapping through an app doesn't feel like it's going anywhere, and there's no one checking in.

Why WhatsApp instead of another app

I didn't want to build one more app that competes for a home-screen slot and gets deleted after a week. WhatsApp is already where most of the people I'm building this for spend their day. One short lesson arrives where you already are, you reply with your own voice, and a real person listens and answers — not an algorithm scoring your pronunciation in isolation.

It also keeps the whole thing small on purpose. No streak-shaming push notifications, no leaderboards. Just a short daily nudge, a real reply, and a small plant that grows when you send a voice message.

Who's behind it

Hangul Habit is a small, founder-run pilot — not a large company. I'm a language educator (I also teach English conversation and interview-style speaking practice professionally), and this project grew out of the same belief that drives that work: short, consistent, spoken practice with real feedback beats long infrequent study sessions almost every time.

Right now it's just me reading every reply and writing every piece of feedback personally — which is exactly why the pilot is capped to a small group while I get the format right.
